# Geospatial

**Summary**: GIS analysis, cloud-native spatial data infrastructure, GDAL/OGR tools, and ArcGIS Pro workflows for geospatial data processing and climate resilience mapping.
**Last updated**: 10-30-2026.

---

- [Introducing Portolan: A serverless spatial data infrastructure](https://cloudnativegeo.org/blog/2026/09/introducing-portolan/): Lebovits (Cloud Native Geo, 09-02) on Portolan, an open specification and toolkit for running an SDI as files in a bucket — GeoParquet and PMTiles for vector, COG for raster, STAC for metadata, no servers, databases or custom APIs. A `rashid` validator, a CLI and agent skills handle conversion, documentation, validation and publication, and every catalog must ship READMEs and `AGENTS.md` so people and agents can use it. Fields of the World (369 TB) already serves 106.6 TB a month this way, and the registry federates independently hosted catalogs without moving the bytes. Related: [[Software]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: Portolan, STAC, GeoParquet, PMTiles, COG, cloud-native geospatial, serverless SDI, object storage, data infrastructure, agents
- *3D Data Science with Python* (Poux, O'Reilly 2025): Book-length workflow for building accurate digital environments from point clouds — acquisition with COLMAP/PyCOLMAP or RealityScan, noise removal, subsampling and feature extraction, mesh/voxel/k-d tree/octree modelling, clustering and semantic segmentation, then Open3D/PyVista visualisation. Notes cover why 3D CNNs struggle with point clouds and how PointNet answers that, voxels as the 3D equivalent of pixels, accuracy/precision/recall definitions, and Postshot, CloudCompare and the City of Vancouver 2022 LiDAR dataset as practice material. Related: [[SPC]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: point cloud, 3D data science, semantic segmentation, PointNet, COLMAP, Open3D, PyVista, voxel, LiDAR, Poux
- [Movement, Water, Connection](https://zenodo.org/records/22979020): Angulo Lopez (Zenodo preprint, 09-26) — a pure-Python circuit-theory connectivity workflow for the Greater Côa Valley, Portugal, built so small rewilding groups without a Julia runtime or clean occurrence data can still run one. Ten focal species grouped by movement medium (land, water, air), Random Forest suitability from GBIF records turned into 100 m resistance surfaces over 10,230 km², current flow solved with a `scipy.sparse` moving-window solver. Adds three locally derived penalties: recency-weighted MODIS fire (10.07% of cells burned 2015–Jul 2025), distance-decayed barriers tiered by observed permeability from 22 field records, and UNESCO heritage areas. The strongest combined corridor runs down the lower Côa to the Douro, and the authors are explicit that 0.78–0.99 training accuracy is not validation. Related: [[Connectivity]], [[Fire]], [[Conservation]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: landscape connectivity, circuit theory, Omniscape, resistance surfaces, Random Forest, GBIF, rewilding, Côa Valley, barriers, reproducible workflow
- [A comprehensive framework to assess multi-species landscape connectivity](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.14444): Prima et al. in *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* replace arbitrary surrogate species with data-driven groups — 193 French vertebrates (52 mammals, 141 birds) clustered on environmental niche plus eight connectivity traits into 11 mammal and 19 bird groups. For each group they run Omniscape circuit theory to map ecological continuities, then derive multi-scale network metrics for 1,619 protected areas, propagating uncertainty through the modelling choices. Protection of continuities and PA connectivity both come out low, and different protection types contribute unequally to group-specific habitat connectivity. Related: [[Connectivity]], [[Conservation]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: multi-species connectivity, circuit theory, network theory, Omniscape, protected areas, ecological continuities, uncertainty, France, vertebrates
- [Detecting old-growth forests from Sentinel-1/2 and geospatial foundation model embeddings](https://github.com/ratsakatika/detecting-old-growth-forests): Ratsakatika, Zotta, Keshav & Lines (arXiv 2609.28194) map old-growth beech–spruce forest across 211,893 ha of Romania's Southern Carpathians from high-confidence, expert-informed parcels. Under spatially blocked validation with 10 km train–test buffers, Sentinel-1/2, AlphaEarth and TESSERA v2 features all lift PR-AUC by 0.21–0.25 over a topography-and-access baseline, so the spectral signal is spatially robust. Unbuffered, TESSERA v2 (128-dim) leads at 0.84, but its edge over Sentinel-1/2 (+0.08) and AlphaEarth (+0.08) narrows to +0.04/+0.03 with intervals consistent with no difference — the headline finding is that buffered spatial validation, not embedding choice, determines whether an old-growth model transfers. At 10 m, 3×3/5×5/7×7 patch CNNs add nothing over pixel-based XGBoost, and predictions agree 81% with a field-calibrated map. The repo ships the full pipeline (public data downloads, label construction, nested blocked CV, 0–30 km buffer sweep, wall-to-wall inference, Carpathian area of applicability) plus Zenodo labels and predictions. Related: [[Forest]], [[Conservation]], [[Geospatial]], [[Software]]. Keywords: old-growth forest, geospatial foundation models, AlphaEarth, TESSERA, Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, spatial cross-validation, area of applicability, XGBoost, Romania
- [City2Graph: Geospatial Graphs for Network Analysis and GNNs](https://city2graph.net/latest/): Python library that turns geospatial data into analysis-ready graphs and converts the same graph between GeoPandas, NetworkX, PyTorch Geometric `Data`/`HeteroData`, and rustworkx. Builders cover urban morphology (buildings + street segments + tessellation within a walking distance), GTFS travel-time graphs, OD matrices, and proximity/contiguity graphs (KNN, Delaunay, Gilbert, Waxman, Queen), plus `add_metapaths` to materialize composite relations for heterogeneous GNNs. Reads Overture Maps, OSM-derived data, GTFS/GBFS directly; PyTorch is optional. Peer-reviewed in *Computers, Environment and Urban Systems* 130:102492 (2026). Related: [[Software]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: graph neural networks, heterogeneous graphs, network analysis, NetworkX, PyTorch Geometric, GeoPandas, GTFS, Overture Maps, OSMnx, urban morphology
- [Exploring multidimensional Zarr datasets with a 3D forecast cube and SQL](https://gjoremilevski.com/posts/zarr-sql-views/): Visual analytics experiment combining spatial and temporal dimensions in one 3D forecast cube — Zarrita runs in a web worker to fetch and decode the Zarr chunks covering the selected region, values are passed as Arrow batches to DuckDB-WASM, and a SQL view exposes lat/lon and forecast hour so cell selections (`value > 28 AND forecast_hour BETWEEN 24 AND 72`) come back as `cell_id`s. Those ids become a mask texture on a custom deck.gl volume layer, so selections stay live while slicing the cube. Demos use ECMWF AIFS forecasts from dynamical.org. Related: [[Software]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: Zarr, deck.gl, DuckDB-WASM, Arrow, web worker, GPU texture, ECMWF AIFS, forecast cube, multidimensional data
- [Mapping and classification of trees outside forests using deep learning](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666017226001483): Lucas et al. in *Science of Remote Sensing* — first multiclass deep-learning mapping of Trees Outside Forests (Forest / Patch / Linear / Tree by FAO area, width, and elongation thresholds) from 20 cm RGB orthophotos across four 100 km² German study areas (Schleswig-Holstein, Brandenburg, NRW north and south). Benchmarks six encoder–decoder architectures: FT-UNetFormer wins (mIoU 0.739, mF1 0.843) and plain U-Net is clearly worst (0.484); transformers beat CNN counterparts and matter most on the structurally hard Patch class. Ablation: nDSM adds +4.4% mIoU, near-infrared adds nothing. Spatial generalization holds up for Forest but degrades sharply for TOF classes, so training data from structurally similar landscapes is essential. Overlaps Copernicus SWF only 26.9% (Linear) and 5.0% (Patch). Code: [TOFMapper](https://github.com/Moerizzy/TOFMapper). Related: [[Forest]], [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: trees outside forests, hedgerows, agroforestry, vision transformers, semantic segmentation, orthophotos, nDSM, coppiced woodland, landscape structure
- [Synthetic Aperture Radar Drone Gets Interferometric Imaging](https://hackaday.com/2026/09/28/synthetic-aperture-radar-drone-gets-interferometric-imaging/): Follow-up on Henrik Forstén's DIY SAR drone. The original build's weak point was GPS at ~1 m; switching to RTK/PPK positioning (fixed ground station corrections in flight, or post-processed against recorded signals) gives centimetre-level trajectory, which is most of why the new autofocus algorithm cleans up the imagery. Also stabilizes the phase-locked loop driving the frequency sweep, adds a fast compression routine to work around the FPGA SD-card bandwidth, and flies the same path twice at two altitudes so phase differences between passes yield an interferogram — an actual elevation map, replacing the constant-elevation assumption that made tall objects lean toward the radar. Related: [[Geospatial]], [[Software]]. Keywords: synthetic aperture radar, SAR, interferometry, RTK, PPK, autofocus, FPGA, elevation mapping, drone, DIY radar
- [From Machine Learning to Large-Scale EO Products](https://ghjuliasialelli.github.io/ML-EO-Maps/): Open online textbook (Sialelli et al., © 2026 IEEE) tracing the whole pipeline from raw satellite data to a published, validated map — six themes covering the EO data landscape, preprocessing (cloud masking, SAR), ML dataset construction and model design, uncertainty quantification, tiled map production and cloud-native dissemination, and design-based accuracy assessment, plus appendices on Sentinel-1 gaps, radiometric terrain correction, and Sentinel-2 cloud masking. My take: good for theory, not practice. Related: [[Geospatial]], [[Software]]. Keywords: Earth observation, machine learning, uncertainty quantification, map validation, accuracy assessment, Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, cloud masking, SAR
- [A1 Collision Density, 2000–2024](https://visquill.com/gallery/uk-a1/): Interactive visualization mapping 25 years of reported road-traffic collisions along the A1 (London–Edinburgh) from UK DfT STATS19 data — collisions projected onto a reference polyline and aggregated per segment into a density histogram, built with VisQuill and MapLibre GL JS. Related: [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: collision density, road safety, STATS19, UK, A1, MapLibre, data visualization
- [Global WPS LULC Static Data: ESA WorldCover 2021 (USGS 24-category, 3 arc-sec)](https://zenodo.org/records/22674626): Updated 2021 global land-use field for the WRF model — ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 cross-mapped by majority vote to the standard USGS 24-category scheme at ~90 m, replacing default 1992-era data; enables genuine fractional land use and improves Noah-MP surface physics (1.5 GB download, includes GEOGRID.TBL). Related: [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: WRF, land use, ESA WorldCover, LULC, Noah-MP, geogrid, 3 arc-second, USGS classification

- [Customize a climate resilience index](https://learn.arcgis.com/en/projects/customize-a-climate-resilience-index/): Build a heat risk index with ArcGIS Pro's Calculate Composite Index tool, combining surface temperature, tree canopy, and population density to target tree-planting for extreme-heat mitigation. Related: [[Geospatial]]. Keywords: ArcGIS Pro, NDVI, vegetation index, arcpy, raster
- [A national-scale sandy beach dataset for India derived from high-resolution satellite imagery and deep learning](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07408-8): A U-Net model trained on IRS ResourceSat-2 LISS-IV (5.8 m) imagery maps sandy beach extents along the entire Indian coastline (IoU 0.84, F1 0.92), delivered in Shapefile format with an accompanying Google Earth Engine app. Related: [[Geospatial]].
