# Insurance

**Summary**: Home insurance markets under climate and wildfire risk, including California's insurance crisis.
**Last updated**: 10-12-2026.

---

- [The U.S. Fisheries Insurance Gap](https://www.insuranceforgood.org/blog/us-fisheries-insurance-gap): Insurance for Good on the structural reasons wild-capture fisheries have no federal insurance program, while crops get one: crop insurance became viable at scale only because the government subsidizes at scale (farmers pay ~38% of premiums; 561M acres covered), whereas fisheries rely on ad-hoc NOAA disaster aid that took a median 3.4 years to disburse (up to 4.8 years) and covered only $2B of the $5B+ in fisheries disaster costs over 30 years. The hazards are also outside existing frameworks — marine heatwaves, harmful algal blooms and hypoxia caused $6.6B in global fishery losses since 1985 and collapsed 90% of the Bering Sea snow crab population, shutting the $227M/yr fishery 2022–2024. Aquaculture is folding into USDA programs (NAP 1994, ELAP 2008, Shellfish Crop Insurance pilot 2024), but wild capture is structurally harder to insure: fishermen don't own the stock, losses arise partly from management closures rather than hazard alone, and stocks move across jurisdictional boundaries — so parametric, community-based and anticipatory designs are being piloted (UCSB EMLab, Ocean Modeling Forum). Related: [[Insurance]], [[Ag]], [[Climate]]. Keywords: fisheries insurance, wild capture, crop insurance, disaster aid, NMFS, marine heatwave, harmful algal bloom, parametric insurance, insurability
- [The value of wetlands in reducing riverine flood losses](https://www.insuranceforgood.org/blog/wetlands-value-riverine-flood-losses): Gourevitch, Gold & Garcia in *Nature Water* link 1985–2023 wetland-area change (National Land Cover Dataset) to riverine flood insurance claims: each hectare lost in a property's own HUC12 sub-watershed raises claim payments ~0.033% (50 ha ≈ +$1,650 on a $100k claim), with upstream effects decaying with distance, while wetland *gains* showed no statistically significant effect — consistent with restored wetlands failing to recover original ecosystem function, so loss is not offset by restoration elsewhere. Cumulatively, wetland loss since 1985 has added $5,915 per claim and $10.1B (9.0% of all riverine payments), concentrated in Houston, southeast Louisiana and coastal Florida and disproportionately hitting lower-income, non-white tracts. With only ~30% of flood losses insured by the NFIP, the true cost may exceed $33B. Argues for pricing upstream wetland change into NFIP rates in place of the Community Rating System's coarse discounts. Related: [[Insurance]], [[Climate]], [[Geospatial]], [[Confinance]]. Keywords: wetlands, NFIP, flood insurance, nature-based solutions, HUC12, National Land Cover Dataset, restoration, actuarial soundness, underinsurance, equity
- [Map shows how much home insurance rates have risen in every California ZIP code](https://www.sfchronicle.com/california/article/home-insurance-rate-increases-data-22306459.php): Stanford research shows the average California homeowner pays 84% more for home insurance than six years ago, with the largest increases in high-wildfire-risk regions like the Sierra Nevada foothills.
