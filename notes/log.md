- 2026-10-22: Ingested "Detecting Romanian old-growth forests" (`geospatial` → Geospatial). The clipping pairs the arXiv paper (2609.28194) with its reproduction repo. Ratsakatika, Zotta, Keshav & Lines map old-growth beech–spruce forest over 211,893 ha of the Southern Carpathians from expert-informed old-growth/non-old-growth parcels, adding AlphaEarth, TESSERA v2 and Sentinel-1/2 features to a topography-and-access baseline and comparing them under spatially blocked validation at 0–30 km buffers. With 10 km buffers all three spectral feature sets lift PR-AUC by 0.21–0.25 over baseline; unbuffered, TESSERA v2 leads at 0.84 but its +0.08 edge over Sentinel-1/2 and AlphaEarth falls to +0.04/+0.03 (intervals spanning zero) at 10 km. Patch CNNs (3×3–7×7) add nothing over pixel XGBoost at 10 m, and predictions agree 81% with a field-calibrated map. The repo documents the full pipeline, provenance records and Zenodo labels/predictions. Linked to [[Forest]], [[Conservation]], [[Software]]. Updated Latest Finds (Geospatial, Insurance, Confinance) and moved the clipping to `processed/`.

- 2026-10-12: Ingested 3 clippings. `insurance` → Insurance: "The U.S. Fisheries Insurance Gap" (Hartley, Insurance for Good, 09-25) — why wild-capture fisheries have no federal insurance program while crops do; crop insurance only works because it is subsidized at scale (farmers pay ~38% of premiums, 561M acres), whereas fisheries depend on ad-hoc NOAA aid with a 3.4-year median disbursement (GAO-25-107076) that covered $2B of $5B+ over 30 years. Marine hazards sit outside disaster finance frameworks entirely: $6.6B in global fishery losses from marine heatwaves since 1985, and the 90% Bering Sea snow crab collapse that closed a $227M/yr fishery for 2022–2024. Aquaculture is absorbed into USDA programs (NAP 1994, ELAP 2008, Shellfish Crop Insurance pilot 2024) but wild capture does not fit the agricultural template — fishers do not own the stock, closures are management decisions rather than hazard, and stocks cross jurisdictions; parametric and community-based designs under development at UCSB EMLab and the Ocean Modeling Forum. "The value of wetlands in reducing riverine flood losses" (Wiley, 08-24) — Gourevitch, Gold & Garcia in *Nature Water* (s44221-026-00656-3) tie 1985–2023 National Land Cover Dataset wetland change to riverine claims: +0.033% of claim payment per hectare lost in the same HUC12 sub-watershed ($1,650 on a $100k claim for 50 ha), upstream effects decaying with distance, and no detectable benefit from wetland *gains* (consistent with restored wetlands not recovering original function). Cumulative $5,915/claim, $10.1B total = 9.0% of riverine payments, concentrated in Houston, SE Louisiana and coastal Florida, disproportionate in lower-income and non-white tracts; likely >$33B once the 70% of losses the NFIP does not cover are counted. Proposes NFIP rate adjustments keyed to upstream NLCD wetland change instead of Community Rating System discounts. `confinance` → Confinance: "Track Carbon Forest Analytics" (Forestry Analytics for Carbon Tracking, beta) — free public platform from the U.S. Endowment for Forestry and Communities, USDA Forest Service and ten forest sector partners standardizing U.S. forest and harvested wood product carbon data; beta tools cover forest stand, landscape and embodied carbon plus a carbon displacement factor, V1 planned summer 2027.
