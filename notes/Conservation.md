# Conservation

**Summary**: Conservation policy, advocacy, and strategy — what makes conservation wins survive political change, and the legal fight over protecting species habitat.
**Last updated**: 10-05-2026.

---

- [How decades of corporate capture enabled today's assault on universities in the United States](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214629626004366): Healy, Eaton, Lachapelle, Roberts, Stephens and Wishart in *Energy Research & Social Science* argue that the current federal offensive is not a break from the past but its escalation — fifty years of corporate-libertarian donors restructuring university *governance*, not just research. The trail runs from the Olin Foundation's $68M "beachhead" build-out of law and economics programs (1974–2005) and the 1971 Powell Memo, to Koch foundations sending $458.7M to 300+ institutions in 2018–2022 with 45% carrying no public grant agreement. The Academic Freedom Index puts US institutional autonomy at 1.7 (from 3.3 in 2019). By early 2026 the administration had initiated 283 of 532 Project 2025 actions, frozen or terminated 7,800+ grants (~$1.4B still unfunded), and settled with six elite universities for over $400M — all against a compliance architecture (Title VI investigations) built by the previous administration. Prescriptions: publish donor contracts, ban nondisclosure clauses, and give faculty real review power over external funding. Kept here for the overlap with the *Durability by Design* note below: the same capture-and-hostile-cycle that erodes institutional autonomy is what makes conservation gains reversible at the next election. Keywords: corporate capture, academic freedom, donor influence, Project 2025, shared governance, climate obstruction, Koch Foundation, Olin Foundation, Heritage Foundation, federal research funding, institutional durability
- [What Are We Doing to Our World of Wonder?](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/11/opinion/trump-endangered-species-act-animals.html): Terry Tempest Williams on the Trump administration's new Endangered Species Act rule, which removes habitat destruction from the statutory definition of "harm" — the one lever the law has over development. DOI calls it preventing environmentalists from "weaponizing" the act; the record is a 99% listing success rate and an estimated 291 species saved. Hundreds of thousands of comments came in, 99% opposed, and 20 states plus DC are suing. Anchored in Great Salt Lake and the eared grebe, whose 30,000 brine shrimp a day disappear with the water. Related: [[Conservation]], [[Climate]], [[Carbon]], [[Measures]]. Keywords: Endangered Species Act, habitat destruction, species listing, Great Salt Lake, eared grebe, legal challenge, conservation law
- [Durability by Design](https://us-nature.com/durability-by-design/): US Nature Initiative's synthesis of 20+ practitioner interviews and dozens of case studies on why conservation wins get undone by the next administration, with four lessons. (1) Appeal to shared values *and* economics, focusing where interests intersect rather than on trade-offs — Organ Mountains–Desert Peaks drew $234M and nearly 4x visitors by 2025. (2) Build coalitions by design; the Republican champion can be the critical variable — fifteen years of work made Boulder–White Clouds politically inevitable and it passed by unanimous consent. (3) Design policies with the people affected, via Safe Harbor-style regulatory certainty for private lands. (4) Plan for implementation, where agency capacity and place-based collaboratives (Blackfoot Challenge) are the actual delivery mechanism. Related: [[Confinance]], [[Conservation]], [[Climate]]. Keywords: conservation strategy, durability, coalition building, policy design, advocacy, Safe Harbor, place-based governance, political durability
